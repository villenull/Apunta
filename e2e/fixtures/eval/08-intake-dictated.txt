Intake for Ana Torres, July tenth, first session, sixty minutes.

So what brings her in. She said the last four months she's been waking up
with, her phrase was "the dread," before anything has even happened in the
day. It's most days, she said five out of seven. Along with that there's a

History. No prior therapy, this is her first time. No psychiatric
hospitalizations, no medications now or ever for mental health. She said her
mother "was a worrier," her word, never treated, never diagnosed as far as
she knows. No substance concerns, she said a drink or two on weekends and
that's been steady for years. Medically she said she's healthy, sees her
primary care doctor annually, last visit was in spring and everything was
fine. She's married, twelve years, describes it as solid, one kid, she's
seven. I asked about safety and she denied any thoughts of self-harm or
suicide, past or present, and she seemed unbothered by the question.

How she presented, she was, um, guarded for the first fifteen minutes or so
and then opened up quite a bit. Articulate, insightful actually, she'd
clearly thought about this before coming in. Anxious but not distressed in
the room.

My formulation. This looks like generalized anxiety, four-plus months,
functionally impairing in the sense that the sleep and the morning dread are
costing her, and the reorg is a plausible precipitant sitting on top of what
sounds like a longstanding anxious temperament and some family loading. No
evidence of a mood disorder, no trauma history she disclosed, no risk
indicators. Good prognosis honestly, she's motivated and psychologically
minded.

Plan. Weekly CBT, we'll start with psychoeducation and self-monitoring, I
gave her a worry log to bring back. I said if we're six or eight weeks in
and the sleep piece hasn't moved we'd talk about whether a medication
consult is worth it, but that's not a now thing. Next session same time next
week.
