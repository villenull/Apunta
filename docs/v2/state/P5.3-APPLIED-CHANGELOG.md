## 6. `OPEN:` lines left in the applied card — 3

All three are inside `P5.3.md` FD13 and all three are recorded rather than
resolved by choosing a number, a word or a key:

1. **The eviction bound for retained disconnected records.** AM-205 fixes the
   retention and the counting but no number, and no repository fact supplies one.
   FD13(e) therefore stands: no TTL, no cap, no age — a retained record blocks
   `ok:true` until a re-register supersedes it or the process restarts. Fail-closed,
   which is the direction AM-205 asked for; an invented bound would have been an
   HS-7 loosening.
2. **The blocker name for a retained unpersisted obligation.** C-UPD@1's client
   vocabulary is fixed and `no_response` is already the server-side word for a
   window that did not answer, but `state/reviews/P5.3-protocol-proof-v3.md` §6 (U5)
   records the blocker vocabulary — and whether `no_response` is a server-side
   word or evidence only — as the owner's and unadopted. The default written into
   the card is to **reuse `no_response`** (no contract change, no vocabulary
   widening); a distinct word would be a C-UPD@1 amendment and is not this card's
   to make. V1 and V9 assert the refusal and its blocker name either way.
3. **What a "re-register" in FD13(c) is matched on.** The protocol proof records
   that a reconnect arrives under a **new instance identity** and discharges
   nothing (§5 (9)); AM-205 says "until a re-register supersedes it" without saying
   what identifies a re-register. The two readings differ in whether a **reload of
   the same tab** discharges an obligation over never-persisted content — a
   data-loss question. The card carries the narrowest key that still lets a
   re-register supersede and instructs the implementer to stop with `BLOCKED`
   rather than widen it.

Ports are **not** an `OPEN:` line: assigning them is the coordinator's dispatch
action under C-ISO@1 rule 2, not an undecided question, and the card now says so.
