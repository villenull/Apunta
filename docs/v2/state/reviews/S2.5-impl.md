### 1. BLOCKING — a Spanish AI failure event is half English, and an es-MX catalogue value is dead code

`server/src/ai/errors.ts:108`

```ts
return msg(locale, MESSAGE_KEYS[code], { banner: UNREACHABLE_MESSAGE });
```

`UNREACHABLE_MESSAGE` (`:62`) is `msg('en', 'ai.unreachable_banner')` — the
**English** constant. `ai.unreachable_banner` has a real es-MX translation at
`shared/src/i18n/es-MX.ts:334-336` that nothing ever renders. Observed at head:

```
--- ai.ollama_unreachable, es-MX ---
Apunta can't reach the local AI — see Setup. Ollama no parece estar ejecutándose en esta computadora.
--- the banner key it should have used ---
Apunta no puede acceder a la IA local: ve a Configuración inicial
```

This reaches the browser as the SSE `error` event for `ollama_unreachable`
(`routes/draft.ts:153`, `routes/chat.ts:290`, `routes/prep.ts:105`,
`routes/brainstorm.ts` and `routes/transcribe.ts:306` all call
`.inLocale(locale)`), and as the 503 body from `routes/transcribe.ts:217-220`.
It breaks the card's objective sentence — "rendered from a catalogue key **in the
language of the request that produced it**" — for the one code whose English is
assembled from another key.

**What makes it pass:** interpolate the *localised* banner —
`{ banner: msg(locale, 'ai.unreachable_banner') }` — and keep
`UNREACHABLE_MESSAGE = msg('en', 'ai.unreachable_banner')` for the log line and
for the base-import sites that still want the English constant. Then add a case
asserting the Spanish `ai.ollama_unreachable` end to end, the way
`locale.test.ts:127` already does for a number-grouped key. This needs no
amendment: `ai/errors.ts` and both catalogues are in May edit.

### 2. BLOCKING — a catalogue-rendered sentence is written to a log line

> (`routes/chat.ts:627-629` `logBlocked` counts, `http/errors.ts:117,133`).

**What makes it pass:** build the `StorageError` for the screen in the request's

Either way the pino line must be English unconditionally. One line, plus a case
that captures the log with the setting at `es-MX` and asserts the line is the
English bytes.

### 11. NOTE — `RawHttpError` carries a placeholder key

`messageIn` as the only reader, would remove the trap.

## The honest verdict on APPROVABILITY

**All four verification rows are `PASS`.** Nothing in V1, V2, V3 or V4 failed,
and I could not make any of them fail. The card's mechanical test is met.

**The card is nevertheless not APPROVABLE as submitted, and I say so bluntly:
the objective "every server-produced sentence a person reads is rendered from a
catalogue key in the language of the request that produced it" is not met inside
the card's own scope, let alone outside it.**

Inside the declared scope, three defects stand, and none of them needs an
amendment — they are in files the card already owns:

- finding 1, a Spanish `ollama_unreachable` that is half English and an es-MX
  catalogue entry nothing renders;
- finding 2, a log line the card's own Must-not-edit says must never carry a
  catalogue-rendered sentence, now carrying one in the request's language;
- finding 3, a user-visible English `outcome_reason` two lines from a sentence
  the card rewrote.

Outside the declared scope, three groups of user-visible English remain
(findings 4, 5, 6), and the card's first stop condition is explicit that a
user-visible server sentence in a file the card does not own is a **`BLOCKED`
return**. The implementer raised two of those three as BLOCKED and was right to;
it did not raise `retractions.ts` at all, and it left finding 3 — which was not
out of scope at all — unmentioned. A card whose own stop condition fires three
times is a card that returned a sentence it could not sign, and a reviewer who
waves that through has not reviewed anything.

So: **CHANGES REQUESTED (attempt 1 of 3).** Fix findings 1–3 and report 4–6
again, and the card is approvable. The English-parity work underneath is
genuinely good — 121 of 122 sentences byte-identical to the base, no English
shipped as Spanish, the guard invariant held on all four files, no threshold, no
scorer, no guard, no logged field and no branch touched, and the tests were added
to rather than loosened — but the deliverable is the sentences, and three of
them are still English on a Spanish install.
