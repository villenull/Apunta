## The crux, answered directly

The coordinator asked for my judgement, not a rubber stamp, on whether AM-045's
line enumeration is the root cause and whether the shortfall is exactly those
four sentences.

**The line enumeration is the root cause, and I would not fix it by extending
the list a third time.** Two reasons, both structural:

1. **Line numbers are not a stable handle, and this card proved it.** The file's
   own line numbers moved *because of this card's edits* — the eight sentences
   became `msg()` calls of different lengths. An amendment that cites lines
   against a moving file is a handle that drifts the moment it is used.
2. **A line list cannot describe a module's output.** `changeSentence` is one
   sentence emitted from six literals across nine lines. No enumeration of lines
   would ever have contained it. Attempt 1 enumerated eight and missed four;
   attempt 2 fixed the eight and found four more. That is not bad luck — it is
   what a line list does.

The right instrument names **what the module returns**, as the coordinator
suggests. Attempt 1's own review said the needed licence was "its sentences",
and AM-045 encoded eight line numbers instead.

So: the objective is **not met**, the shortfall is **exactly those four
sentences and not wider**, and the coordinator's reading — that any residual
English reaching a person is blocking under the card's first stop condition —
is **correct**, and I say so plainly: **one of the four is the first line of the
most common refine outcome in the product.**
