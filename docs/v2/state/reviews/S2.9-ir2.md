## Defects

### N1 — The eight lock-takers are not the eight racers: `brand.spec.ts`'s `afterAll` writes the row, and the card forbids touching it

- **ID and location.** `docs/v2/state/dispatch/S2.9-ir.md`, §Fixed decisions,
  decision 1, fifth bullet (`:257-264`) against §May edit (`:164-168`).
- **The text that conflicts, or exactly what is missing.** The bullet closes with
  a completeness claim: "The tests that take it are the two in
  `settings-appearance.spec.ts:21` and `:77`, the one at `workspace.spec.ts:548`,
  and the five in `brand.spec.ts:293-333` — eight, all of them racers or
  asserters of that row, **none of them a bystander**." Eight tests is the right
  count of *tests*; it is not the set of *writers*. Inside the very same describe,
  `brand.spec.ts:301-311` reads the theme row in `beforeAll` and
  `brand.spec.ts:313-333` **PUTs it** in `afterAll` (`:315`,
  `await request.put('/api/settings', { data })`) with no lock held. And §May
  edit forecloses the obvious repair in the same breath: "Its existing
  `beforeAll`/`afterAll` restore at `:301-333` is the model, not the thing to
  rework." So the one theme writer the card names is one an implementer is told
  to leave exactly as it is. Before the repair this was implicit — the old text
  said "every spec that writes or asserts the theme row takes it", which a file
  satisfies through its hooks. The repair narrowed the obligation from specs to
  eight enumerated tests, and the hooks fell out of the set.
- **One concrete failure scenario.** In V1's amplification `brand.spec.ts` runs
  at `--repeat-each=3`, so three describes each fire an `afterAll` PUT of the
  theme value read by their own `beforeAll`. `settings-appearance.spec.ts:21`
  holds the lock, has clicked Light and is at `:60` or `:65` asserting
  `data-theme="light"`. A brand `afterAll` PUTs `theme=<whatever the row said
  when that describe started>` in the middle of it. The appearance test fails
  with exactly the recorded race — while every one of the eight lock-takers
  behaved precisely as the card specifies, and no stop condition describes it:
  decision 4's escalation path is written only for `:63-65` still flaking, and
  V1's Expected cell calls a missing assertion a FAIL but says nothing about an
  extra writer. The implementer's reachable responses are all closed.
- **One specific correction or named prerequisite.** Say what happens to the
  `afterAll` PUT. Either (a) name it as a ninth and tenth lock participant and
  say how a `beforeAll`/`afterAll` pair takes a test-scoped fixture — the honest
  shape is a small exported `acquire`/`release` helper in `support/fixtures.ts`
  that the describe calls around its whole block, which is still "a new
  isolation helper … added beside the existing ones" and so inside May edit;
  or (b) state the residual honestly: that the restore is deliberately outside
  the lock, that it is a narrow window, and what a worker must do if V1 or V3
  flakes on it — a named message and a report, not a timeout and not a config
  change. Either sentence closes it; silence does not, because the card's
  completeness claim is what an implementer will rely on.

### N2 — The lock's cost is never stated, and V1's own amplification queues 24 UI tests behind it under a 30 s test timeout

- **ID and location.** `docs/v2/state/dispatch/S2.9-ir.md`, §Fixed decisions,
  decision 1, "held for the duration of the test" (`:222-224`) and the bounded
  retry bullet (`:251-253`), against V1 (`:420`) and §Must not edit (`:191-195`).
- **The text that conflicts, or exactly what is missing.** The repair named the
  primitive and its five properties; it did not name the primitive's **cost**, and
  the cost is not free here. `e2e/playwright.config.ts` sets no `timeout`, so
  every test runs under Playwright's default — `playwright/lib/common/index.js:524`,
  `var defaultTimeout = 3e4` — and fixture setup, where a lock wait lives, is
  inside that budget. V1's first invocation is `--repeat-each=3 --workers=4` over
  `settings-appearance.spec.ts` (2 lock-takers), `workspace.spec.ts` (1) and
  `brand.spec.ts` (5): **24 acquisitions against one lock file**, each held for a
  multi-navigation UI test (`brand.spec.ts`'s five each do `openHome` — two API
  calls, a `goto`, two assertions — plus `painted()` and `assertPainted()`, and
  the zoom test adds a CDP emulation). Twenty-four such tests serialised is on the
  order of a minute of lock time; the tail of that queue waits past 30 s and fails
  on timeout while holding nothing. The card closes both remedies: §Must not edit
  forbids `test.slow()` and timeout inflation, and decision 1 forbids narrowing
  the hold below "the duration of the test". The bounded-retry bullet asks for a
  deadline and never says what it should be, so the deadline is the implementer's
  to guess and any value they pick either fires early on a loaded box or is set
  above the timeout that will fire first.
- **One concrete failure scenario.** The implementer does exactly what the card
  says — a fixture that polls `fs.mkdirSync` every 50 ms against a 20 s deadline,
  released in teardown. V1 passes every assertion and fails on
  `Test timeout of 30000ms exceeded` in `brand.spec.ts` or
  `settings-appearance.spec.ts` on whichever copy reached the queue last. The
  honest conclusion — "the sanctioned fix cannot pass its own row" — is correct
  and unhelpful, and the two next moves are Stop condition 2
  (`playwright.config.ts:88`) and the forbidden timeout. V3 is under far less
  pressure (the same eight tests, un-amplified, twice over because the `es-MX`
  project re-runs every spec at `playwright.config.ts:108-113` and shares the
  lock through the same `APUNTA_DATA_DIR`), which is exactly why the cheap
  localiser would fail while the expensive acceptance might pass.
- **One specific correction or named prerequisite.** Give the wait a budget in
  the card, in one sentence, with the number: either state that the acquisition
  deadline must sit inside the 30 s default test timeout and say what happens to
  the tail when the queue is longer than the budget, or relax the hold from "the
  duration of the test" to the window that touches the row (the PUT and the
  assertions that read it), which is a per-spec judgement the card can delegate
  explicitly. Failing both, reduce `--repeat-each` for the lock-takers in V1 and
  say why — the amplification's job is to reproduce the *overlap*, and 24
  serialised acquisitions test the lock's queue, not the race.

### N3 — Fixed decision 5 and Step 2 contradict Fixed decision 1 about `plan.spec.ts`

- **ID and location.** `docs/v2/state/dispatch/S2.9-ir.md`, §Fixed decisions,
  decision 5 (`:330-336`) and §Steps, step 2 (`:398`), against decision 1's fifth
  bullet (`:257-264`).
- **The text that conflicts, or exactly what is missing.** Decision 5: "Its setup
  does write shared state (`:70-79`), so it **must** take whatever isolation the
  other writers of those rows take, and that is the whole of its change." Step 2
  repeats it as an instruction: "Add decision 5's participation to
  `plan.spec.ts:58`." But no other spec takes any isolation for the three rows
  `plan.spec.ts:70-79` writes. The formats race was answered by **scoping inside
  `formats.spec.ts`**, not by any lock, and no spec locks or scopes anything for
  patients or notes — `formats.spec.ts:56` is the only `toHaveLength` in `e2e/`,
  and `plan.spec.ts` writes a format named `uniqueName('E2E plan format')` plus
  a patient and three notes under that patient, so nothing global can see them.
  The obligation therefore resolves to nothing, while the one sanctioned lock in
  the card is reserved: decision 1 says the fixture is taken by exactly eight
  tests and "nothing else in the suite does". Both instructions cannot be
  obeyed. This predates AM-088, but the repair sharpened it — decision 1's
  enumeration turns "every spec that writes or asserts" from a file-level rule

- **One concrete failure scenario.** The implementer reads step 2 as an

  add it to `plan.spec.ts:58` — a test that never touches the theme row — which
  contradicts decision 1's "eight, none of them a bystander", adds a 25th
  acquisition to N2's queue, and gets `e2e/playwright.config.ts` no closer to
  correct. The alternative, editing nothing, leaves step 2's "add …" visibly
  undone and the worker reporting an instruction they could not carry out.
- **One specific correction or named prerequisite.** One sentence in decision 5
  replacing "whatever isolation the other writers of those rows take" with the
  finding: its format is uniquely named and its patient and notes are its own, so
  no scoped or global assertion in the suite can observe them, and **its change is
  none** — or, if the owner wants participation for symmetry, name the mechanism
  and drop "nothing else in the suite does" in decision 1. Step 2's "Add
  decision 5's participation to `plan.spec.ts:58`" then has to change with it.
