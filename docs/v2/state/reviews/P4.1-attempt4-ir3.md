### 1.3 The thing I was asked to try: a fixture that satisfies the wording yet still passes unfixed

**I could not construct one, and the reason is structural rather than lucky.**
Requirement 5 is self-policing: the case is required to fail when the fix is
reverted, so a fixture that passes unfixed cannot satisfy requirement 5 and the
implementer is forced to change the fixture. Transcript §6 lists the four
nearest-miss shapes and what each does:

| Candidate | Unfixed | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `?%25zz=1` (the `URLSearchParams` shape, written by hand) | returns `['%zz']`, no throw | **Vacuous** — but it is the exact trap the card names and forbids at `:434-436`, and it fails the control. |
| `?Expires=1&%zz=1` | **throws** | Legitimate alternative, non-vacuous. Satisfies requirements 1-3. |
| `?%25=1` (a well-formed escape whose *decoded* text is a bare `%`) | returns `['%']`, no throw | **Vacuous** — but `%25` is not "a bare malformed escape" in the fixture, so requirement 1 excludes it, and it fails the control. |
| `?%53ignature=v` (valid escape decoding onto an admitted name) | returns `['Signature']` | Not malformed at all; excluded by requirement 1. The residual ambiguity is narrow and worth naming: "a bare malformed escape" in
requirement 1 is a property of the **fixture**, and requirement 3's "present
**undecoded**" is a property of the **result**. `%25zz` and `%25=1` are the two
strings where those two properties can be read as satisfied in the looser
direction. Requirement 1's own wording ("a **bare** malformed escape") excludes
both — in `%25zz` the escape is well-formed and the literal text is a decoded
`%`; in `%25=1` likewise — and requirement 5 closes it absolutely. **Say plainly:
I could not build a vacuous fixture that satisfies the card as written.**
