## IR-01 — DEFECT

### D1 — The objective is not reachable through the authorised write scope

- **ID and location:** `docs/v2/cards/S2.10.md`, §Objective (dispatch
  `S2.10-ir.md:65-93`) against §Fixed decisions 1 and 4 (`:262-330`, `:348-382`)
  and §Stop conditions 2 and 5 (`:605-610`, `:622-630`).

- **The text that conflicts, or exactly what is missing:** the Objective is
  "The e2e line at **default workers** exits 0, five times in a row", and V3
  says "five consecutive runs, every `exit=0` — this is the row's whole point".
  But Fixed decision 4 states the residual in its own words and then declines
  to remove it: "With decision 1 in place, the remaining exposure is exactly
  this: another spec's page lists a patient this card's specs created, and its
  recency fetch for that patient lands after the undo deleted it", followed by
  "both out of scope here", and "**If any remain at V3, that is Stop condition
  5**".

  Decision 1 does not close that window, and it is worth being exact about why,
  because decision 2 could be read as claiming it does. Decision 1 changes
  **which batch is deleted** (`halaxy-import.spec.ts:76-80`, `.first()` → the
  captured id). It does not change **that a delete happens**, and the delete
  still strands a sibling page. The pinned timeline in
  `docs/v2/state/reviews/S2.9-impl.md:244-252` shows both authors doing it:

  ```
  00:54:42.386  POST /api/import/claude/run              ← creates …7039, …703a
  00:54:42.448  POST /api/import/batches/…-7038-…/undo   (Claude batch, own id)
  00:54:42.553  GET  /api/patients/…-7039-…/notes  → 404
  00:54:43.099  POST /api/import/batches/…-70a1-…/undo   (Halaxy batch, own id)
  00:54:43.124  GET  /api/patients/…-70a2-…/notes  → 404
  ```

  Both undos are already **their own batch**, and both still 404 a bystander's
  recency fetch. The victim is structural, not a wrong-batch artefact:

  - every workspace page lists **all** patients —
    `server/src/db/patients.ts:43-58` has no `LIMIT`, and
    `web/src/routes/Workspace.tsx:142-143` hands the whole list to
    `usePatientRecency`;
  - `usePatientRecency` fires one `GET /api/patients/<id>/notes` per listed
    patient, six at a time, skipping only ids already in its module-level
    `cache` (`web/src/hooks/usePatientRecency.ts:22-24`, `:45`, `:69`), and the
    imported patients sort to the top of the queue because they are the most
    recently edited;
  - the fetch is therefore **already in flight** when the undo lands — 105 ms
    and 25 ms after the delete in the log above — and `requirePatient`
    (`server/src/routes/notes.ts:36-38`) turns it into a 404 that
    `consoleErrors` (`e2e/support/fixtures.ts:70-85`) attributes to whichever
    test owned the page (`plan.spec.ts:58`).

  Nothing in May edit touches any of those three links. May edit is
  `halaxy-import.spec.ts`, `import.spec.ts`, `spelling.spec.ts` and additions
  to `e2e/support/fixtures.ts`; Must not edit forbids
  `web/src/hooks/usePatientRecency.ts` by name, forbids `plan.spec.ts`, and
  forbids `consoleErrors` "in any form … a guard narrowed for the whole suite".
  The exposure also **grows across V3's five runs**, because they run in one
  sandbox and every non-import spec leaves its patients behind, so run 5's
  storm is larger than run 1's — the same accumulation the card itself names as
  the spelling flake's S1 mechanism (`S2.9-impl.md:213-215`).

  So a fully correct implementation of decisions 1–5 inside May edit will still
  leave V3 red on any run where a victim page's storm overlaps an undo, and the
  card's own tripwire (Fixed decision 7: "a single green run cannot distinguish
  a fix from luck") makes a lucky 5-of-5 the only other exit. That is **fail on
  a correct implementation**.

  To be fair to the card: it is not hiding this. AM-093
  (`docs/v2/state/AMENDMENTS.md:116`) authorised it knowing the boundary —
  "every recency 404 is counted and **a remaining one is a stop**, since a
  `usePatientRecency` or `consoleErrors` change is the owner's". So the defect
  is not a gap in the instructions; it is that **the authorised scope cannot
  deliver the authorised objective**, and the card will most likely end
  `BLOCKED` at Stop condition 5 rather than `APPROVED`.

- **One concrete failure scenario:** V1 is green and decision 1's two identity
  assertions hold, because in V1 neither import spec has created anything while
  the amplified `plan.spec.ts` copies are loading their pages. V3 run 3 then
  goes red at `plan.spec.ts:58` with `GET /api/patients/<id>/notes → 404`; the
  worker counts it as decision 4 and Stop condition 5 require, and stops. The
  implementer did nothing wrong, the objective is not in reach, and S2.R stays
  blocked.

- **One specific correction or named prerequisite:** the owner decides
  **decision 4's lever (c)** before S2.10 is dispatched, and the answer is
  recorded as an amendment that widens May edit. The realistic options, in the
  card's own terms:

  1. **Production (recommended).** `web/src/hooks/usePatientRecency.ts` — stop
     treating a 404 on the recency read as a page failure: skip the patient the
     list can no longer vouch for, or have `GET /api/patients` carry the recency
     the server already knows. Decision 4(c) names this as "a product change in
     `usePatientRecency`"; it needs its own card, because S2.10 changes no
     production behaviour.
  2. **Guard narrowing, the owner's to take.** A `consoleErrors` allowlist
     scoped to exactly `GET /api/patients/<id>/notes` 404s. Must not edit
     forbids it here and correctly says so; HS-7 forbids loosening a guard to
     make a result pass, so this needs an explicit owner amendment naming the
     exact predicate, not a widened fixture.
  3. **Restate the objective.** The owner amends the tripwire to "no
     wrong-batch 404 and no `Nothing new to import.`, with every recency 404
     counted and reported" — which is the shape of AM-092's own relief for
     S2.9. §6 reserves rewriting an acceptance row to the owner, and Fixed
     decision 4 already forbids this card inventing it.

  Whichever is chosen has to arrive **before** implementation, because decision
  4 says "Do not invent a fourth lever" and Stop condition 5 says the choice is
  the owner's.

### D2 — `waitForTimeout(500)` at `halaxy-import.spec.ts:56`: forbidden and permitted in the same card

- **ID and location:** `docs/v2/cards/S2.10.md`, §May edit bullet 1 (dispatch
  `:188-192`) against §Fixed decision 4(b) (dispatch `:361-367`).

- **The text that conflicts:** May edit says, of `halaxy-import.spec.ts`,
  "Nothing else in the file: not the format POST at `:23-25`, not `uniqueName`
  at `:44`, not the patient-list detour at `:54-71` **and not the
  `waitForTimeout(500)` at `:56`**, not the `checkScreen` calls, not the
  undone-line assertion at `:81-83`." Fixed decision 4(b) says "The one piece
  of fat is the `waitForTimeout(500)` at `:56`; … if they show it is not,
  **removing a sleep is not a weakened assertion**", and decision 4 closes "So
  the card fixes (a)'s *author* half, **leaves (b) to the one change it
  permits**". Decision 4(b) asserts a permission May edit denies, and HS-9 makes
  the May edit list binding.

- **One concrete failure scenario:** Step 1's traces show the 500 ms is not
  load-bearing. The implementer reads "the one change it permits" as permission
  and deletes `:56`. V2's `git diff --name-only {{BASE}}` stays clean — the file
  is a May-edit path, so nothing in the card's own verification catches it — and
  the change comes back as a review finding against an explicit prohibition.
  The mirror image exists too: an implementer who honours May edit has "left
  decision 4(b) undone", which is the shape of defect earlier reviews on this
  plan have had to chase.

- **One specific correction or named prerequisite:** pick one. Either drop
  `and not the waitForTimeout(500) at :56` from May edit (decision 4(b) is
  conditional, so leaving the sleep is always permitted), or drop decision
  4(b)'s permission and say plainly that the run-to-undo window is not reducible
  here. The first is one deleted clause and costs nothing.

  A related, softer observation: decision 4(b) aims at the **wrong interval**.
  The exposure in the pinned timeline is *delete → victim fetch* (25–105 ms),
  not *run → undo*. Shortening the run-to-undo window does not shorten that gap,
  and deleting the patient sooner arguably widens the chance the victim's queue
  still holds it. Lever (b) is unlikely to be the one that works.
