### IR-02 — DEFECT (blocking): FD2b's direction mapping is inverted, and a bare negator vocabulary cannot express a lost negation

**Evidence, from the S3.1 tuning gold this card says it mirrors.** For a *lost*
negation the detectable artefact is the affirmative restatement, and the gold puts
it in `mustNotContain`; the negated fact sits in `mustCapture`:

- `eval-es/tuning/lost-negation/01-sin-dormir.txt`: `mustNotContain` = `"volvió a dormir bien"`, `"ya duerme bien"`, `"sueño (restaurado|normalizado|resuelto)"`, `"insomnio (resuelto|corregido|desapareci[óo])"`, … — no negator token anywhere; `mustCapture` = `sueno-no-restaurado` with `any` `["no ha vuelto a dormir bien", "no ha vuelto a dormir", "sigue sin dormir(?: bien)?", …]`, plus a `safety`-tagged `autolesion-ausente`.
- `eval-es/tuning/invented-negation/03-medicamento-diario.txt`: `mustNotContain` = `"niega(?:ndo)?"`, `"no refiere"`, `"no reporta"`, `"jam[aá]s"`, `"no est[áa] medicad"`, `"sertralina (suspendida|retirada)"`, … — the negator vocabulary, qualified.

FD2b states: "the sidecar decides the direction of the error (`mustNotContain` for
a lost negation, `mustCapture` for an invented one)". That is the opposite of the
gold in both halves:

- An **invented** negation is a negation in the note that the source does not
  support. It is caught by `mustNotContain` (the gold's invented-negation
  fixtures put it there). FD2b routes it to `mustCapture`, where a match is
  *credit*, so an invented negator would score as a captured fact.
- A **lost** negation is a negation in the source that the note dropped. There is
  nothing in the note for `mustNotContain` to match — the negator is the thing
  that is *absent*. It is caught by the affirmative restatement in
  `mustNotContain` plus the un-captured `mustCapture` fact. FD2b routes it to
  `mustNotContain`, where negator terms would instead fire on **every faithful
  Spanish note**: "no ha vuelto a dormir bien" contains `no`, so `mustNotContain:
  ["no", "nunca", …]` would ban the negation the corpus exists to preserve.

That second half is the one that matters: FD2b as written makes every correct
Spanish note a fabrication, on the Spanish corpora whose negation handling is the
card's reason for existing.

**Exact bounded fix**, replacing the second and third sentences of FD2b (the
direction sentence and the "vocabulary, consumed per fixture" sentence):

> The lexicon supplies **vocabulary only**, never a direction and never a
> detector. Which way the error runs is stated per fixture by the sidecar's own
> keys, and the two run in opposite directions, exactly as in the S3.1 gold:
>
> - **invented negation** — a negator in the note that the source does not
>   support — is `mustNotContain`. The `lexicon: "negation"` key expands into the
>   alternation those patterns are written over, so the sidecar authors
>   `"niega(?:ndo)?"`, `"no refiere"`, `"no reporta"`, `"jam[aá]s"` and the rest of
>   its own list as before, and the expansion supplies the negator vocabulary
>   inside them.
> - **lost negation** — a negator in the source that the note dropped — is **not**
>   detectable from negator vocabulary at all: the negator is what is missing. It
>   is `mustNotContain` over the *affirmative restatement* the note wrongly
>   asserts, plus a `mustCapture` fact whose `any` patterns are the negated form.
>   A control or fixture in this class therefore carries `lexicon: "negation"` on
>   its `mustCapture` fact, where the expansion supplies the vocabulary of the
>   negated form, and its own restatement patterns in `mustNotContain`.
>
> `mustNotContain` never receives a bare negator term. A negator on its own
> matches every faithful Spanish note, and the corpus exists to keep those
> negations.

This stays inside the owner's Choice 2 as recommended: vocabulary through the
sidecar, `score.ts` still free of a negation rule and of any guard import, and
A3's "no general lost-negation check" intact.

### IR-03 — DEFECT (blocking): `expect` has no predicate, so V2's expectation is uncomputable

**Evidence.** FD6's key table gives `expect` ∈ `flagged | pass | fail` with
positive→`flagged`, clean→`pass`, empty→`fail`, degenerate→`fail`, and a load-time
`CorpusError` on a mismatch. FD6's report table prints `positive: n flagged of n`,
`clean: n passed of n`, `empty: n failed of n`, `degenerate: n failed of n`. No
definition of `flagged`, `pass` or `fail` appears anywhere in the proposal.

There is a substantive gap, not only a missing sentence. `NoteScore.gating` is
built at `score.ts:388-395` from exactly six conditions: `!s1`, `!s2`,
`collapsedNote`, `bannedHits.length > 0`, `gatingF6`, `gatingNovel`, and
`contextFull`. A `mustCapture` fact that is not captured lands in `capturedFacts`
and in `safetyMisses` (`score.ts:342-349`) and costs `c1`/`c2` points — it adds
**no** `gating` entry. Under IR-02's corrected direction, the lost-negation
control's lexicon-driven detector *is* a `mustCapture` fact, so "the positive
control is flagged" and "the positive control's negated fact went uncaptured" are
different observable states, and V2's "7 positive flagged" cannot be checked
against either without a stated predicate.

**Exact bounded fix**, adding one row to FD6's key table and one sentence under
it:

> | `expect` | yes | `flagged` \| `pass` \| `fail`, derived: positive→`flagged`, clean→`pass`, empty→`fail`, degenerate→`fail`; a mismatch between `control` and `expect` is a `CorpusError` at load |
>
> **The three words are predicates on the control's `NoteScore`, stated here so
> no implementation can disagree:**
> `flagged` ⇔ `gating.length > 0`; `pass` ⇔ `gating.length === 0`; `fail` ⇔
> `gating.length > 0` **and** the class's named mechanism produced it. Each `fail`
> class names its mechanism in its own sidecar: `empty` ⇔ `gating` contains
> `S4 every section blank` (the `collapsedNote` branch, `score.ts:302, 391`);
> `degenerate` ⇔ `gating` contains `F6 unsupported conclusion`, which requires the
> sidecar to set `f6: "gating"` and to list the repeated boilerplate's section in
> `noConclusion` so the existing F6 path supplies it (`score.ts:389-390`). A
> control whose `expect` is `flagged` and whose `gating` is empty is a **missed
> control** under FD6's admissibility rule; a `fail` control whose named
> mechanism is absent is also a missed control, because the class did not test
> what it claims to test.

And V2's expected cell, to match:

> …and **10** es-MX controls. All ten behave as FD6's predicates state: 7 positive
> `flagged` (`gating.length > 0`), clean `pass` (`gating.length === 0`), empty
> `fail` on `S4 every section blank`, degenerate `fail` on
> `F6 unsupported conclusion` — each named mechanism printed beside its line.

### IR-04 — DEFECT (blocking): a control's `source` is unspecified, and without one the `clean` control cannot pass

**Evidence.** `scoreNote` takes a `Fixture` and reads `fixture.source` for six
checks: `sourceForLexicon(fixture.source)` feeding F7's novelty test
(`score.ts:270, 496-505`), `numberFidelityFlags(noteText, fixture.normalisedSource)`
(`score.ts:315, 600-603`), `medicationFlagsFor(...)` (`score.ts:316, 611-622`),
`quotedSpanViolations(noteText, fixture.normalisedSource)` (`score.ts:314, 562-570`),
`novelContentWordRate`, and `expansionRatio` (`score.ts:677-681`). FD6 gives a
control `controlNote` — "the exact note text the control is scored on" — and never
says what the control's `source` is, while FD3's nested inventory makes each
control's `.txt` a real file on disk.

Both plausible readings break a row:

- **`source` empty.** Every number in the note is absent from the source, so F3
  flags every number; every quoted span violates; and — decisively — F7 tests each
  lexicon entry with `entry.pattern.test(stemmedBody) && !entry.pattern.test(stemmedSource)`
  (`score.ts:499-501`). With an empty source the second term is true for every
  term, so any diagnosis or risk term in the note becomes `gatingNovel` and pushes
  `F7 novel diagnosis/risk term` into `gating` (`score.ts:323, 393`). A `clean`
  control — a faithful note, which is exactly the note most likely to name a
  diagnosis or a risk — is then `flagged`, and V2's "clean passed" fails on a
  correct implementation.
- **`source` = the `.txt`, and the `.txt` is the note.** Then the file's role is
  undefined against `controlNote`, which is stated to be the note; FD6's `must be
  shaped as the fixture's own sections` applies to `controlNote` and not to the
  `.txt`.

**Exact bounded fix**, replacing FD6's `controlNote` row and adding one key row:

> | `controlSource` | yes | the transcript the `controlNote` claims to be a note of, as a file `<class>/<name>.txt` in the controls directory, in the same shape as a corpus fixture's transcript. It is the control's `fixture.source`. It is **not** the note and never appears in `controlNote`. |
> | `controlNote` | yes | the exact note text the control is scored on; `""` for the `empty` class, a header-only / repeated-boilerplate body for `degenerate`, a faithful note for `clean`, and a note that commits the class's error for `positive`. Must be shaped as the fixture's own sections. |
>
> `controlSource` exists because `scoreNote` compares the note against the source
> in six places (F2 quoted spans, F3 numbers, F4 medications, F7 novelty, the
> novel-content-word rate, the expansion ratio — `score.ts:270, 314-316, 496-505,
> 600-622, 677-681`). A control whose source is empty is not a control: F7 finds
> every diagnosis and risk term novel and gates, so the `clean` control could not
> pass. Each `controlSource` therefore contains the same clinical vocabulary its
> `controlNote` does wherever the control is not *about* introducing it: the
> `clean` control's source and note agree on every term, the `lost-negation`
> control's source carries the negation its note drops, the `invented-negation`
> control's source does not. The `empty` control's `controlSource` is a real
> transcript and its `controlNote` is `""`.

### IR-05 — DEFECT (major): controls are not excluded from `ModelReport.scores`, so they reach `sensitivity()`, and the inadmissible exit code is unpinned

**Evidence.** `renderReport` takes `models: readonly ModelReport[]` and
`ModelReport.scores: readonly NoteScore[]` (`report.ts:18-22`), and every corpus
aggregate in the report is computed from those scores. `cli.ts:271` then computes
`sensitivity(result.models)` over the *same* array, and `deflects` is
`bannedStrings > 0 && gatingConclusions > 0 && filledBlanks > 0 && cleanFixtures > 0`
(`run.ts:200-216`). FD6 says controls "get their own table in the report, four
lines with their own denominators beside it", and V1 asserts corpus percentages are
identical with and without the controls directory. Neither statement keeps a
control's `NoteScore` out of `ModelReport.scores`.

If a control's score lands in `scores`, then the seven `positive` controls — which
are *designed* to be flagged, three of them via `mustNotContain` — contribute
`bannedStrings` and `gatingConclusions`, and the `clean` control contributes
`cleanFixtures`. `deflects` can then become true on a corpus that is entirely
clean, which is precisely the outcome `cli.ts:277-283` exists to catch ("A fake
run that reports everything clean is a blind harness"). C-EVAL@1 §3's "nothing it
scores counts" would be satisfied in letter and defeated in effect: the CI
self-check would go green on the controls' evidence rather than the harness's.
`CLAUDE.md` records `npm run eval -- --fake` as the CI self-check that "passes when
the scorer *deflects* on the canned notes", and FD1 additionally claims "provider
mode with no new flag behaves exactly as it does at the base commit: same
prompts, same corpus handling, **same report**" — which is already untrue once a
controls table is added, and is the second half of the same leak.

Separately: FD6 says a missed control "exit[s] non-zero" and V3 expects "exit
non-zero". `cli.ts` uses `2` for an unknown flag (`cli.ts:110-116`) and
`process.exit(gated ? 1 : 0)` for a real run (`cli.ts:288-289`), and the fake
branch exits `0`/`1` (`cli.ts:282, 285`). "Non-zero" cannot distinguish an
inadmissible run from a measured gating failure, and V3's expected result cannot be
checked against a digit.

**Exact bounded fix**, three bounded edits:

1. In FD6, after "Controls are outside every corpus denominator", add:

   > A control's `NoteScore` is **never** placed in `ModelReport.scores`. The
   > controls loader returns a separate `controls: readonly ControlScore[]`, the
   > report renders it as its own four-line table from that array alone, and
   > `sensitivity()` at `cli.ts:271` is computed from `result.models` exactly as
   > it is at the base commit. This matters and is asserted: `deflects` is
   > `bannedStrings > 0 && gatingConclusions > 0 && filledBlanks > 0 && cleanFixtures > 0`
   > over those scores (`run.ts:200-216`), so seven positive controls in that
   > array would supply the banned strings and the gating conclusions that make a
   > blind harness "deflect" — the one outcome `cli.ts:277-283` exists to catch.
   > V1 asserts `sensitivity(models)` is byte-identical with the controls
   > directory present and absent, not merely that the corpus percentages match.

2. In FD1, replace "same prompts, same corpus handling, same report" with:

   > …same prompts, same corpus handling, and the same corpus block of the report.
   > The report additionally carries the controls table FD6 specifies, and
   > `--controls none` suppresses it; `--controls` defaults to
   > `server/src/eval/fixtures-controls/<locale>`, so the default `npm run eval
   > -- --fake` self-check loads the `en` controls, which FD6 states as
   > deliberate.

3. In FD6's admissibility bullet and in V3's expected cell, replace "exit
   non-zero" with "**exit 3**":

   > A missed control is checked **before** the `--fake` deflect branch
   > (`cli.ts:270-286`) and exits **3** — a code no other path returns — naming the
   > control's key and its class. `1` stays the measured gating signal
   > (`cli.ts:288`), `2` stays the usage refusal (`cli.ts:114`), `0` stays a clean
   > measured or deflecting run. A CI red is then diagnosable from the digit
   > alone, and V3's expectation is checkable.

Nothing here relaxes a gate: `3` is a *new* code on a path that already failed.

### IR-06 — DEFECT (major): the `es-MX` number/unit/medication lexicon cannot be built as specified

**Evidence — three separate problems in FD2 and FD2c.**

*Where the terms live.* FD2 says number words, units and medications come from
`docs/research/es-mx-clinical-glossary.json`, `category ∈ {number, unit,
medication}`, "using each entry's `es_mx` as the term". FD2c says the loader is
locale-scoped and "`--locale es-MX` loads **only**
`server/src/eval/lexicons/es-MX.txt`". §B-negation says the same file holds "One
`term<TAB>category` per line" with "Categories this card uses: `negation`,
`number`, `unit`, `medication`". So the 152 entries are read from the glossary at
scoring time, or materialised into a file at authoring time; the proposal says
both, and they are different implementations. Note also that the glossary itself
is under Must-not-edit, which is fine for reading but settles nothing.

*The terms cannot be whole tokens.* FD2c requires "each lexicon entry is matched
as a **whole token after `stem()`** — never as a substring". Measured over the
glossary: of 152 entries in the three categories, **65 are not single tokens** —
`number` 24 of 51 (`"veintiuno ... veintinueve"`, `"cien / ciento"`, `"uno / una"`,
`"dos veces"`, `"medio / media (seis y medio)"`), `unit` **8 of 8** (`"mg
(miligramo)"`, `"mcg / µg (microgramo)"`, `"g (gramo)"`, `"mL (mililitro)"`,
`"unidades (U)"`, `"UI (unidades internacionales)"`), `medication` 33 of 93
(`"ácido valproico"`, `"aspirina (ácido acetilsalicílico)"`, `"Zoloft
(sertralina)"`, `"Prozac (fluoxetina)"`). `\b` + `escapeRegExp` + `\b` over
`mg (miligramo)` compiles and never matches a note saying "5 mg"; over `"uno /
una"` it never matches "uno". Every unit entry is inert, and F4's medication flags
lose a third of the vocabulary.

*The category filter and the load-time error collide.* FD2c widens the filter to
"`CATEGORIES` … plus `negation`, `number` and `unit`" and makes "a line whose
category is outside the widened set a **load-time error**". The glossary has five
further categories — `presentation` 92, `intervention` 65, `risk` 45,
`pattern-marker` 31, `frequency-time` 14 — so if the glossary is read at load time
every one of those 247 lines is a load-time error; if the file is pre-filtered,
the widened filter is only load-bearing for the file and its own wording no longer
describes what happened.

**Exact bounded fix**, replacing FD2c's first and second sentences and FD2's
number/unit/medication sentence:

> **FD2 (source of the Spanish terms).** The three lexicons are built **once, at
> authoring time**, into `server/src/eval/lexicons/es-MX.txt` from
> `docs/research/es-mx-clinical-glossary.json`, filtered to
> `category ∈ {number, unit, medication}` — 51 + 8 + 93 = **152** entries,
> verified present. Each entry is split into **atomic terms** before it is written:
> on `/`, on `(`/`)` and on ` … `, and each resulting piece is trimmed. A piece
> that is still not a single token (`\w`-only, accents included) is **not written**
> and is listed in the file's header as excluded with its reason. That leaves 95
> usable terms and excludes 57 — the counts are asserted by a test against the
> glossary, and the excluded list is in the file so a reviewer can check the
> provenance without the glossary. Nothing is read from the glossary at scoring
> time and no entry is added to that file. The negation entries are authored
> separately, per §B-negation, and are the only entries with no glossary source.
>
> **FD2c (loader).** The loader is locale-scoped: `--locale en` loads only
