### 4. Ownership residual — closed, and the mechanism is real here

**Can a foreign process satisfy the socket-owner assertion?** No, and the
reason is structural rather than procedural. The assertion is that the listening
socket on the test port is owned by the spawned pid, read from
`/proc/net/tcp` matched against `/proc/<pid>/fd` (or `ss -ltnp`). The residual
the card names — a third party binding the port in the window between the
free-port check and our `listen` (`:294-299`) — cannot survive the new
assertion: there is at most one listener on a port absent `SO_REUSEPORT`, and
the server does not set it, so if a foreign process held the port our `listen`
would fail with `EADDRINUSE` and our process could not be the responder at all.
The one thing a foreign process *could* do — hold the port and answer — is
exactly what the assertion excludes, because the socket inode it would own is
not in our pid's fd table.

**Is the mechanism real on this platform?** Yes, and it is the standard Linux
mechanism. A listening TCP socket appears in `/proc/net/tcp` with `st = 0A` and
its inode in the inode column; `/proc/<pid>/fd/*` symlinks read
`socket:[<inode>]`. Matching the two yields the owning pid. `ss -ltnp` prints
the same fact directly (`users:(("node",pid=…,fd=…))`). I confirmed `ss` is
present at `/usr/bin/ss` (iproute2-7.2.0) and that `/proc` and `/proc/net/tcp`
are readable here.

**Readable without new privileges?** Yes. `/proc/<pid>/fd` is mode-restricted to
the process's own uid, and the process in question is the one the test itself
spawns as the same user — so the read succeeds without `sudo` and would
*fail* for an unrelated user's process, which is the desirable direction. The
card's alternative (`ss -ltnp`) has the same property: process details appear for
sockets the caller owns. Nothing here needs a capability, a setuid helper or a
new package.

**Inside the card's May edit?** Yes. The assertion is implemented in
`scripts/v2/package-linux-resources.test.sh`, listed `(new)` at `:90-91`, and
the mechanism reads only `/proc`, which no repository file governs. It needs no
edit to `boot-error.ts` (which `:270-272` correctly notes is not editable) and
none to any contract.

**And the lock half it joins is the production one, not an invention.** I
checked the committed `server/src/platform/data-lock.ts`: `LOCK_FILENAME =
'apunta.lock'` (`:45`), `processStart` documented as "Linux: field 22 of
`/proc/<pid>/stat` — start time in clock ticks since boot" (`:25-26`, read at
`:102-113`), and `appVersion: APP_VERSION` written at `:326`. The startup order
the card relies on is real: `server/src/index.ts:33` `acquireDataFolderLock`
precedes `:47` `openDatabase`, which precedes the catch at `:52` and
`serveBootError` at `:71` — so the lock is written before `/api/health` can
answer, with no race. The card's own caution is also right and worth keeping: a
clean release unlinks the lock, so the read must happen while the process is
alive.
