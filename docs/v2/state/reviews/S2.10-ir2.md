## Is the AM-094 allowlist exactly one class, and provably narrow?

**One class: yes.** The predicate admits a message only if all of three hold — the
browser's own resource-load text naming a 404 (never a text that merely mentions one,
never any other status, never a `pageerror`); the URL parses, is the app's own origin,
is a `GET`, and its pathname matches `^/api/patients/<uuid>/notes$` exactly, with no
query, no further segment, no other sub-resource and no other route; and the status is
404 read from the message rather than inferred from the URL. That is a single class, and
the two classes nearest it are explicitly refused by name: `net::ERR_ABORTED` from the
hook's own abort controller (`usePatientRecency.ts:73`, `:77`) and every other status
on that path. The fence around it is as good as this plan asks: the exact prohibition
list in Must not edit, the same list in Stop condition 6, and V2's diff check on the
fixture itself.

**Provably narrow: yes in mechanism, once R1 and R2 are settled.** The proof is the
required table, and it is a good proof because four of its six cases can only be
expressed if the predicate really takes the URL — a 404 on another route, a 500 on the
admitted path, a near-miss path, and a foreign origin. That forecloses the widest
possible degradation, a predicate that matches on the text alone. Two clauses the
predicate states but the required table does not exercise are noted below (N3).
