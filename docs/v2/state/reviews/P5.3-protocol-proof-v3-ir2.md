## Findings

**1 — MODERATE, disclosure. A socket close deletes the window record and with it
every obligation it carried, and the model nowhere says so; a dirty disconnect
before a quiesce is indistinguishable from a clean close.**
`drop` (proof:270–282) does `this.windows.delete(k); this.polls.delete(k)` with no
quiesce-relative qualification and no record of what the window held.
`B1-i`: w1 edits `note-1` (never flushed), `drop('w1')`, then
`initiate('shell-stdin')` → **`{ok:true,blockers:[]}`** with the epoch fence up,
while `stored` holds nothing for `note-1`; my independent oracle at that instant:
*"storage does NOT hold `w1|i1`'s content on note-1"*. The random sweep hit this
shape **143 times in 500 schedules**. `B1-v`: after a reconnect the fresh instance
gets a new identity and the old buffer is nowhere. **Classification — and why it is
not a safety defect against what is claimed:** the header's S1 (proof:11–16) and
the report's §2 are both scoped to what the server *holds* ("no window held content
the store does not hold"), and a dropped window is not held; nothing in the report
asserts anything stronger, so no stated guarantee is false. It is the same
abstraction limit as §5 limit 4, which already discloses that a renderer holding an
unsent **audio** buffer is invisible and that "the client freeze/ack work is an
adoption prerequisite this report may not credit itself with" — limit 4 simply
never says the same about **typed text**, and §2.1's narrowing ("a statement about
content still held") reads as if a holder could not simply disappear. It is also in
tension with ir1 finding 5's own repair: refusing a *note switch* was the right
answer to "a buffer fell out of the model for good", and `drop` reaches the same
state by another door, unrefused. **Correction (report prose only, one clause in
§5):** state that `drop` deletes the window record and every obligation on it, that
a disconnect with unpersisted content therefore reads as a clean window until the
client reconnects under a *new* instance identity, and that the client half is the
P5.4 adoption prerequisite exactly as for audio. **Minimal model change if the
owner wants it stated in behaviour rather than prose:** keep the record with
`disconnectedAt` and let `unsavedTruth` range over disconnected-but-present
records until a re-register supersedes them.

**2 — DOCUMENTATION. "First-writer-wins" is wrong twice over; the behaviour is
last-writer-wins and the safe direction, so nothing else moves.**
`settleFlush` writes `this.stored.set(nk, …)` unconditionally (proof:378–381), so
the second save of a note replaces the first. Proof:84 says "A store that holds one
content per note is a **first writer wins** slot" and the report repeats it in §6
(line 170) and §7 (line 190); proof:129–131 says the opposite ("last-writer-wins"),
and report §2.2 (lines 92–97) correctly says the second write replaces the first.
**Classification: wording, not behaviour.** `B2` measures it: w1 then w2 both save
`note-1`; the store holds `i2|note-1|1`, and the outcome is
`{ok:false,['save_error','conflict']}` with `truth.unsaved === 1`. An overwrite can
never be a success, because a content identity embeds its instance id, so two
windows can never hold the same identity on one note and a conflict always
accompanies an overwrite. Delete "first-writer-wins" from proof:84 and from report
lines 170 and 190 and keep §2.2's sentence, which is the accurate one.

**3 — DOCUMENTATION. "The epoch fence refuses every public mutation" is stronger
than the code, and the overstatement is harmless.**
`drop` is in `PUBLIC_EVENTS` (proof:90–92) and is **not** freeze-guarded:
`C1` shows `drop` returning `dropped` under a held success, with `windows` 2→1,
and five drop/re-register cycles never lifting the fence (each re-register is a
`frozen_no_registrants` *refusal*, queued — `deferredRetriesQueued` 5). **This is
a harmless monotone removal, not a weakened safety claim:** the success guarantees
are about the windows that remain, removing a window can only delete entries from
`conflictingNotes()` and from the blocker sets (all empty at a success), and nothing
new can become owed. I checked it rather than assuming it — the invariant battery
runs after `drop` (proof:555–565, `batteryRuns` counted in every sweep run) and
never fired, which is direct evidence that a drop cannot invalidate a held success.
`closeRequest`/`closeAnswer` are likewise unlisted (`C1`: under the fence the re-arm
is refused, the window cannot be asked, `closeRequest` → `defer_no_wait`, and with a
poll forced held the close still decides `close` while moving no note state). Fix
the blanket sentence in the header (proof:23) and report line 26–27 to say
"every mutation that could invalidate the success", or list `drop` explicitly as a
monotone removal. Note also that the close path's one real mutation —
`this.decisions.push` and `pendingClose` (proof:516, 533) — is **bookkeeping, not
application state**: nothing about the guarantee, the fence or any window record
depends on it. On the coordinator's question of discriminating: that is the right
discrimination, and "the decision path mutates nothing" is accurate in the sense
that matters.

**4 — DOCUMENTATION, low. The structural battery assertion cannot prove the list is
complete, and the gap is real though benign.** `F12` (proof:1218–1221) checks that
each name in `PUBLIC_EVENTS` is wrapped; it cannot detect an omitted name.
Deriving the list independently, the mutating prototype methods **not** in
`PUBLIC_EVENTS` are `bind`, `touch`, `armSweep` and `admitFlush`. Calling
`admitFlush` directly spends the token, pushes the attempt and mints the job with
only one battery run, and that run comes incidentally from the wrapped `begin` it
calls, not from the token spend itself (`C1`/B6). No schedule reaches any of these
as a public boundary, and the report already words the assertion as "every *listed*
event", so this is a limit to state rather than a hole. Suggested sentence in §5:
the battery covers the public event list, not every internal mutator.

**5 — DOCUMENTATION, low. §2.3's remedy for a two-tab conflict is incomplete.**
Report lines 98–101 say the drain "reports `save_error`/`conflict` and the therapist
closes one tab". Closing the *offending* tab is exactly the drop of finding 1 — it
discards that tab's unpersisted buffer without a word — and closing the *other* tab
does not release the drain either: `B1-iv` measures both orderings and the next
generation still returns `{ok:false,['save_error','no_response']}`, because the
surviving window's own buffer is still owed. The protocol fails closed in every
case (`B4`: neither conflicting window can be closed on a clean-looking answer —
both return `confirm_recording`/`defer_unsaved`, never `close`), so this is a prose
correction — say that closing a tab resolves nothing by itself and that the
surviving buffer must still be flushed — not a safety finding. **Close handling does
not, and the model cannot, recommend discarding a conflict**: it has no such
recommendation, and my probes confirm the close path refuses the unsafe window in
both directions.

## Coordinator's specific concerns, answered

- **Store: first- vs last-writer-wins.** Last-writer-wins in the code; the
  "first-writer-wins" phrase is wrong in two places and §2.2 is right. Safe
  direction, measured (`B2`). Documentation finding 2.
- **Same-buffer supersession narrowing.** **Legitimate, and I tested it rather than
  accepting the framing.** `B3`: the later content of one buffer is the only thing
  owed and it *is* still owed (`flush` of rev 1 is `flush_replayed_token`, and a
  failed save of the current content still settles `save_error`); a *different*
  window's content is never forgiven (`B3-b`: `save_error` + `conflict`); a
  *different note* in the same window is never forgiven (`B3-c`); and a window
  holding two notes at once carries **both** obligations (`B3-d`: `w.held.size === 2`,
  keys `note-1`, `note-2`). The narrowing is narrower than it looks in one respect:
  it also excuses a *failed* save of an intermediate revision, which `F2b`'s own
  comment (proof:837–840) now concedes. That is consistent, and it is disclosed.
- **Drop erasing obligations.** Yes — finding 1, with the four positions the
  coordinator asked for: **dirty before quiesce → `{ok:true}`** (finding 1);
  **during the quiesce → `no_response` at t = 2000** (`B1-ii`, inside the untouched
  30 s bound); **after a clean report but before settlement → `{ok:true}`, which is
  correct**, because a substantiated clean report already discharged every
  obligation and `stored` really holds `i1|note-1|1` (`B1-iii`) — the model cannot
  produce a dishonest post-report success, since `consistent()` requires
  `o.unsaved === unsavedTruth(w)`; **after a conflict rollback and into the next
  generation → `save_error` + `no_response`** (`B1-iv`), the surviving buffer still
  owed; **reconnect to the same note → `save_error` for the new instance's own
  content, the old one's nowhere** (`B1-v`).
- **Oracle with one buffer per instance and `left[]`.** Confirmed as a real
  limitation of the *proof's* oracle, and my probes go past it. `B5` runs a
  multi-note window with a **return to a previously written note** (the returned-to
  buffer is owed again, `ok:false`, then flushes) and a **store overwrite** by a
  second window (model `{ok:false,['save_error','conflict']}`, oracle: *"storage does
  NOT hold `w1|i1`'s content on note-1"* — the two agree). `B1` and the sweep treat
  a drop as a deletion, so the oracle's blind spot does not mask finding 1; the
  sweep's 143 drop-with-owed-buffer count is exactly that category, counted
  separately rather than hidden.
- **"Every public mutation fenced" while `drop` mutates under the fence.** Finding 3:
  harmless monotone removal, false-stronger prose. Decided, with evidence.
- **Battery using `unsavedTruth` itself, and only some schedules.** The battery
  does use `unsavedTruth` (proof:590) — it is the model's own definition, so it can
  only catch *internal inconsistency*, never a wrong definition; that is exactly why
  my oracle never touches it. The "only some schedules" gap **is** closed:
  `batteryRuns` after every listed event including refusals (`C4`: 9 refusals, 9
  runs), and my 500-schedule sweep accumulated **4670 battery runs** with no
  violation. Timers do not bypass it (`B7`: the `DRAIN_MS` callback and the sweep
  callback each ran the battery).
- **Structural event list.** Finding 4: cannot prove completeness; the gap is named.
- **`b.saveOutcome` "obsolete".** **It is not.** It is a live getter
  (proof:222) read at proof:447 and proof:593, and D1 is not defeatable by a later
  attempt: an errored attempt settles its own generation immediately
  (`{code:'settled'}`, `['save_error']`), so no second attempt can overwrite the
  recorded outcome — I checked the one route that could have (`reissue` after an
  error → `frozen_no_reissue`). No finding.
- **Malformed boundaries within supported JSON values.** `C10` fires 315 junk calls
  — `undefined`, `null`, booleans, `0`, `42`, `-1`, `1e21`, `''`, `'x'`, `[]`, `{}`,
  `[1,2]`, `{a:1}`, `new Date(0)` — at 23 public boundaries each, including
  `noteId`, `headers`, `query`, `outcome`, `jobId` and the whole answer object. No
  throw, no window or binding created, and the honest success is still reachable
  afterwards. `C7`/`C8` cover the stdin path before and after a lift is defined,
  with a state fingerprint. Good.
- **Completion IDs, reverse orders, late error, jobs across generations, 30 s and
  per-binding 2 s.** `B3` (both outcome directions, before and after the genuine
  completion), `B7` (per-binding bound fires; deadline callback runs the battery),
  `B8` (a refine spanning three generations, `timeout` twice, then clean; rollback
  ends no work, exactly one completion; a live wait survives 5× the per-binding
  bound and only the 30 s bound decides it, at exactly `t = 30000`). All preserved.
- **Client-recording honesty, no invented production guarantee.** `C9`:
  `w.recording` is written only by `publish`, and the only recording-related method
  on the prototype is `recordingTruth`; `status()` keeps `claims` and `truth`
  separate; a report cannot erase the claim; the P5.4 half is named as an adoption
  prerequisite. §5.7/§5.8 keep the scratch and the non-self-acceptance honest. No
  invented guarantee found.
- **Adapted probes.** I did **not** inherit the conclusion. I read the adaptation
  header (A1–A6) and the bodies of `X1`, `X3b` and `X4`: each adaptation is forced by
  an API the corresponding finding changed (a completion must now name its operation;
  the prescribed code is `stale_completion`; a fenced publish leaves the battery
  nothing to catch; deferred registrations are a disclosed limit), and each body
  still asserts the counterexample's **substantive** claim — `X1` keeps
  `unsavedTruth(w2) === true`, `w2` at revision 1 while storage holds revision 2, and
  `outcome.ok === false`; `X4` keeps "a buffer is not abandoned" and that the honest
  success stays reachable. Cosmetic only: the probe *titles* still assert the old
  defect text. Where an adaptation drops an assertion (A3, A4) the mutation evidence
  restores it independently — `M5`/`M14` die on `F12`, `M8` on `S9`+`F14`. Legitimate.
