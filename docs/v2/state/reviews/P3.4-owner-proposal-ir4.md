## N1 — BLOCKER — §C.2 step 9 manufactures the condition §C.2 step 10 fails on

§C.2 step 9 requires, after the warp and the ±1 px read-back:

> **After the warp and the ±1 px read-back, wait 600 ms — two poll intervals —
> so the hover transition the warp itself caused has flushed** … Then **re-read
> the target's rectangle from the live buffer** and recompute `T` from it.

and gives the reason:

> hovering a patient row inserts a tooltip into the DOM … if the pointer is still
> inside the tooltip, `elementFromPoint` returns a non-interactive `div` and the
> note never opens.

**That reason is false as shipped.** `web/src/styles/app.css:4511-4527`:

```
4511: .project-tip {
4512:   position: fixed;
4526:   pointer-events: none;
4527: }
```

`.project-tip` is `position: fixed` (so no reflow of anything) and
`pointer-events: none` (so it can never be `elementFromPoint`'s result and can
never intercept a press). Nothing about the tooltip can make the click miss.

**But the tooltip does something else, and that is the blocker.** The tooltip's
own text leaves are published as click rectangles. `web/src/components/PatientsColumn.tsx`:

- `:1173-1175` — `onMouseEnter` → `setTip({ id, rect: event.currentTarget.getBoundingClientRect() })`
- `:1425-1444` — `createPortal(<div className="project-tip" …><div className="project-tip-name">{tipPatient.name}</div>…</div>, document.body)`
- `:1435-1436` — `left: rect.right + 20`, `top: rect.top`

`div.project-tip-name` has no element children and its trimmed `innerText` is the
patient's name — so it satisfies `readRects`'s text-leaf rule exactly
(`web/src/main.tsx`, baseline `:99-111`: `children.length === 0`,
`1 <= label.length <= 64`). It is therefore published as a rectangle whose label
is **the same string** as the row's `span.name` the harness is clicking.

So the sequence §C.2 mandates is:

1. warp onto the `John Smith` row → `onMouseEnter` fires → the tooltip is
   inserted into `document.body`;
2. wait 600 ms so the insertion **has flushed** (that is the step's stated
   purpose);
3. re-read the frame from the live buffer — which now contains **two** live
   rectangles labelled `John Smith`;
4. §C.2 step 10: *"If more than one live rectangle in the selected frame carries
   the label, the step `FAIL`s naming the count and the global indices, because
   the target in that frame is ambiguous."*

Step 4 fires. The patient-open click — which gates `(d)`'s whole handler half —
fails by construction, on a correct build, whenever the tooltip's leaf is inside
the published set.

**How conditional is it?** The tooltip is portaled to `document.body`, so it is
last in document order, and `readRects` breaks at `rectCap = 200`. Whether its
leaf is published depends on how many qualifying text leaves the workspace view
has before it — **unmeasured, and not measurable without a run** (U-8 below). That
does not soften the finding: the document mandates creating the condition and
mandates failing on it, and offers no rule for the case either way. It is the
same shape as IR-03 (a check that cannot pass) and AM-183's charge.

The author was aware of the two leaves — §C.2 step 10 says *"`tagName` alone
separates the two `John Smith` leaves in play — the row's `span.name` from the
tooltip's `div.project-tip-name`"* — but that resolves only the **`clickEl`
comparison** (§C.2 step 10's later sentence). It does not touch the **selection**
rule two sentences earlier, which is the one that fails. The two halves of the
same step disagree.

**Exact remedy — select by the rectangle's global identity, not by its label.**
This is the identity §E already introduces, and it is stable here precisely
because the portal is appended last in document order:

> §C.2 steps 9 and 10, replace "re-read the target's rectangle from the live
> buffer" with: **record the selected rectangle's `frame`-qualified global index
> when it is selected, and re-read that index after the hover flush** — the
> hover's own DOM insertion adds leaves at the **end** of document order (the
> tooltip is portaled to `document.body`), so an already-published leaf keeps its
> global index. Require that index to still be present in the post-hover frame and
> still to carry the label; if it is not, re-select **once** by label, and if that
> re-selection is ambiguous, `FAIL` naming the count and the global indices as
> now. **A duplicate label is not by itself a failure**: `rectTag` distinguishes
> the two (`SPAN` for the row's leaf, `DIV` for the tooltip's), and the tooltip
> cannot be the click target in any case (`pointer-events: none`,
> `web/src/styles/app.css:4526`). The ambiguity rule stays, for a frame in which
> **two rectangles with the same `rectTag`** carry the label.
>
> And delete step 9's parenthetical rationale, replacing it with the true one:
> **the 600 ms wait and the re-read exist because hovering inserts new text
> leaves into the published rectangle set, so the frame the target was selected
> from is stale — not because the tooltip can intercept the press, which
> `pointer-events: none` forbids.**

Nothing else in §C.2 moves: the one re-warp, the one click, the 30 s window, the
10 s caps and the `clickEl` three-field comparison are untouched, and the
comparison gets *stronger*, because `rectTag` now does selection work as well.

## N2 — BLOCKER — §E's snippet labels each batch once, so the new rule cannot see the mixture and cannot ever complete

§E's harness snippet, exactly as written:

```js
const entry = batches.get(batch) ?? { batch, total, frame, rects: new Map() };
```

`??` evaluates the object literal **only on first insert**. `total` and `frame`
are therefore captured the first time a batch index is ever seen and **never
refreshed**. Two consequences, both demonstrated
(`docs/v2/evidence/P3.4/proposal-ir4/frame-identity-adversarial.mjs`):

**(a) The mixture D1 was raised for passes.** D1's scenario is a hover that
inserts and removes a text leaf, so `total` is unchanged. After publication
`f1` (5 batches) the entries all read `frame: f1`. Publication `f2` overwrites
batches 0–3's *values* but leaves every label at `f1`. Judged against the only
frame label that exists:

```
A) mixed publication read mid-arrival, total unchanged
   as written (?? create-once)  ask for f1 -> 5/5 batches, all frame f1   (batches 0-3 actually hold f2 values)
   refresh on sight             ask for f2 -> refused: batch 4 missing/stale
```

A read holding four batches of `f2` and one of `f1` is accepted as a complete,
consistent frame. That is exactly the defect §E was written to remove, and §E's
own prose — *"a frame is complete only when every batch of one publication is
present and every one of them carries that publication's `frame` and `total`"* —
is not what the snippet computes.

**(b) Completeness becomes unreachable.** Once the batch count ever grows during
a run — `total` 200 → 220 gives `ceil(220/40) = 6` batches — batch 5 is created
with `frame: f2` and batches 0–4 keep `frame: f1` **forever**, because nothing
ever refreshes them and the hook publishes only on change:

```
B) count crosses a batch boundary (200 -> 220, six batches)
   as written (?? create-once) -> batch 0 missing/stale (frame=f1)   [labels: 0:f1 … 5:f2]
   refresh on sight             -> 6/6 batches, all frame f2
```

The completeness loop then restarts forever and §E's rule turns into a permanent
`FAIL` ("incomplete frame: 5 of 6 batches") on a page that is publishing
perfectly. The proposal offers no staleness escape for this, because §E's
"what is not claimed" paragraph is about *staleness*, not about a frame the
read can never accept.

Control: a single complete publication passes under both readings, so this is
not a general breakage — it is specific to the case the mechanism exists for.

**Exact remedy — refresh the label on every sighting:**

```js
const entry = batches.get(batch) ?? { batch, total, frame, rects: new Map() };
entry.total = total;   // ← the frame identity is the LAST publication's, not the first's
entry.frame = frame;
batches.set(batch, entry);
```

Then: a mid-publication read sees `f2 … f2 f1` and is refused; a complete
publication sees `f2` in every batch and is selected; and because `sendRects`
republishes **all** batches on every change, liveness holds (every batch is
refreshed by the same publication). Verified: both adversarial scenarios flip to
the correct answer with the two added lines.

Two smaller things §E should say in the same edit, since the implementer needs
them and neither is stated:

- the harness's rectangle regex must widen from `^i(\d+)_(x|y|w|h|l)$` to
  `^i(\d+)_(x|y|w|h|l|t|d)$` for `rectTag`/`rectTestId` to survive — it is
  *shown* in §E's snippet but never named as a change;
- with refresh-on-sight, "the same `frame`" is a rule about **the most recent**
  publication of each batch index, and §E's "what is claimed" sentence should say
  "the most recent" rather than "that publication's", so the reader cannot
  re-derive the create-once reading.

## N3 — BLOCKER — §T.2's fixture cannot be written, so two of its five tests never reach the guard

§T.2 gives the helper with a defaulted id and says, in terms:

> `cardText` keeps its own `# T1 Test card` H1 while the file is named after
> `id`; that is harmless and **must not be "fixed"** — `loadPlan`
> (`docs/v2/tools/plan-lib.mjs:208-217`) keys the card by **file name**, so a
> fixture written as `P3.4.md` is addressed as `P3.4` even though its H1 still
> reads `# T1 Test card`.

Both halves are wrong, and the "must not be fixed" instruction is what makes it
load-bearing. `loadPlan` does not key by file name; it **parses the id out of the
H1 and then enforces that it equals the file name**:

```js
// plan-lib.mjs:118-120
const titleMatch = /^# (\S+) (.+)$/.exec(lines[0] ?? '');
if (!titleMatch) throw new Error(`${file}: first line must be "# <ID> <title>"`);
const [, id, title] = titleMatch;

// plan-lib.mjs:213-215
const card = parseCard(readFileSync(join(planDir, 'cards', file), 'utf8'), file);
if (`${card.id}.md` !== file) throw new Error(`${file}: file name must be ${card.id}.md`);
```

The range `:208-217` is right; the characterisation is not. And `loadPlan` runs
at `build-dispatch.mjs:135`, **before** the attempt guard at `:158-171`, so the
throw pre-empts it:

```
A) file=P3.4.md  H1="# T1 Test card"   -> exit 1   Error: P3.4.md: file name must be T1.md
B) file=P3.4.md  H1="# P3.4 Test card" -> exit 0   - Attempt 5 of 3, plus two corrective attempts …
C) file=T1.md    H1="# T1 Test card"   -> exit 0   - Attempt 4 of 3, plus one corrective attempt …
```

Run against §T.2 exactly as written, with the patched tool of §T.1 applied:

| §T.2 test | asserts | actually gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — `--attempt 5`, no exception | exit 2 | **exit 2** ✓ |
| 2 — `--attempt 5 AM-nnn` for `T1` | exit 2 | **exit 2** ✓ (fixture id `T1`, H1 matches) |
| **3 — `--attempt 5 AM-nnn` for `P3.4`** | **exit 0** | **exit 1** ✗ — uncaught `loadPlan` throw |
| **4 — `--attempt 6 AM-nnn` for `P3.4`** | **exit 2** | **exit 1** ✗ — same throw, guard never reached |
| 5 — `--attempt 4 AM-nnn` for `T1` | byte-identical line | **passes** ✓ |

Test 3 is the **only** test that demonstrates the grant works. Test 4 is one of
the two §T.2 calls "the ones that matter: without them the grant is an unfired
promise" — it is the test that shows **there is no sixth**. Both are written
against a fixture that cannot exist, and §T.2 instructs the implementer not to
correct it.

**Exact remedy — one extra defaulted parameter, and the "must not be fixed"
sentence replaced:**

```js
const card = (command, expected, id = 'T1') => `# ${id} Test card
…
function makePlan(cardText, reviewRow = '| T0.R | T0 | Test review | L2 | extra checks |', id = 'T1') {
  …
  writeFileSync(join(dir, 'cards', `${id}.md`), card(command, expected, id));
}
```

and in §T.2's prose: *"the fixture's H1 id follows `id`, because `loadPlan` keys
the card by the **H1** and then enforces `card.id + '.md' === file`
(`plan-lib.mjs:213-215`); a fixture whose file name and H1 disagree makes
`loadPlan` throw before the attempt guard is reached, which is exit 1 and not the
exit 2 tests 2 and 4 assert."*

With that, all five of §T.2's tests pass as specified, and so do nine further
adversarial cases I ran against the same patched tool (C-10). §T.2's other two
D3 repairs — `{ cards: { [id]: 'APPROVED' } }` and the 25 = 9 + 4 + 12 count —
are correct and verified.

## M2 — DEFECT — §C.2's own arithmetic does not fit the 30 s window it forbids widening

§C.2 step 5 says each per-move freshness wait "is capped at **10 s**, measured
per move, and the cap is spent out of time the method already spends — **no
timeout is widened** (the row's 45 s first-fact window and its 30 s click window
keep their current lengths…)". Step 11 forbids "widening of the 30 s window". §E
puts the frame-completeness loop "**within the click's own 30 s window**".

An all-succeeding worst case inside the click window:

| phase | worst case |
| --- | --- |
| step 5, calibration target 1 (`ptrN` wait) | 10 s |
| step 5, calibration target 2 (`ptrN` wait) | 10 s |
| solve + `getmouselocation` read-backs (§C.2 steps 5–9) | ~0.5 s |
| step 9 hover settle | 0.6 s |
| step 10 `clickN` wait | 10 s |
| **total** | **≈ 31.1 s > 30 s** |

plus §E's completeness loop, which runs in the same window and shares its budget.
The two constraints cannot both hold: either the calibration waits are charged to
a budget of their own — which is a new phase budget, and step 5's sentence is
what forbids it — or a cap has to come down, and step 5's sentence is what fixes
it at 10 s.

**Exact remedy — name the window, and make the arithmetic fit.** Say in step 5:
*"the two calibration waits are charged to a calibration phase budget of its own,
`2 × 10 s`; the 30 s click window is **not** widened and applies to each of the two
clicks exactly as now, so no existing timeout changes length"* — and either drop
the clickN cap to 8 s (16 + 0.6 + 8 = 24.6 s, with headroom for the read-backs
and §E's loop inside 30 s) or state that the clickN wait is bounded by the
remainder of the click window rather than by a second 10 s. The 45 s
first-fact window is not available: §A.3's gate has already consumed it before
(a) returns.
