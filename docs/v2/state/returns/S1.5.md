## Headline findings (detail in `docs/research/es-mx-spellcheck.md`)

- **Q1.** `dictionary-es-mx@2.0.0` is the only Mexican-Spanish Hunspell pair on
  npm. Declared data licence `(GPL-3.0 OR LGPL-3.0 OR MPL-1.1)`; unpacked
  879,564 B over 7 files, of which the data is `index.aff` 158,014 B +
  `index.dic` 714,044 B; published 2023-11-03, untouched since. Built by
  `wooorm/dictionaries` from `sbosio/rla-es` (the same build LibreOffice,
  OpenOffice and Firefox use). No `@cspell/dict-es_mx`, no `dictionary-es-419`,
  no `es_MX` in `hunspell/hunspell`, no `mozilla/dictionaries` repo.
- **Q2. FITS, through the one pre-approved exception, elected MPL-1.1.** The offer
  is disjunctive and free ("Puede seleccionar libremente"), the data is offered
  under an MPL option of a multi-licence, and it ships unmodified in its own two
  files — every element L-POLICY's exception requires. Obligations that follow,
  quoted from MPL-1.1: §3.1 ship a copy of the licence with the source form;
  §3.5 the Exhibit A notice beside the data; §3.6 a conspicuous notice that the
  source form is available and where; §3.7 the MIT app and the MPL word list may
  ship as one product. §3.2/§3.3 are drafted around Modifications, which Apunta
  would not create — the reason "shipped unmodified" is load-bearing. The
  wrapper's MIT covers only the wrapper and does not touch the data.
- **Two mechanical consequences for S6.1.** (a) `npm run lint` fails the moment
  the package enters the lockfile — `collect-licenses.mjs` forbids any licence
  string containing GPL/LGPL unless the arm is registered in
  `DUAL_LICENSED_CHOICES`; the election must be recorded there and in
  `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.md` + `docs/decisions.md`. (b) The published tarball
  most likely does not include the licence file, so Apunta must carry the notice
  and the MPL-1.1 text itself.

  spell check off does not fire on licence grounds. C-ES-GATE is untouched —
  Spanish stays owner-gated, and this card says nothing about dictionary quality.
